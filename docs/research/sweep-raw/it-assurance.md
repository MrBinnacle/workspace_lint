## Findings

**1. XCCDF defines exactly the outcome vocabulary this project needs — and it splits `incomplete` into four.**
CLAIM: `resultEnumType` allows `pass`, `fail`, `error` ("an error occurred and test could not complete"), `unknown` ("could not tell what happened, results with this status are not to be scored"), `notapplicable` ("Rule did not apply to test target"), `fixed`, `notchecked` ("Rule did not cause any evaluation by the checking engine"), `notselected` ("Rule was not selected in the Benchmark, and therefore was not checked"), `informational`. · SOURCE: XCCDF 1.1.2 XML Schema / NIST / https://csrc.nist.gov/schema/xccdf/1.1.2/xccdf-1.1.2.xsd (fetched; docstrings verbatim) · RELEVANCE: The four-outcome model is under-specified. XCCDF separates *rule does not apply to this target*, *rule never evaluated*, *rule out of scope*, and *evaluation attempted and failed*. Those four have different user remedies. Also: `unknown` is explicitly excluded from scoring — the coverage manifest needs the same rule, so non-verdicts never silently enter a denominator. · VERDICT: ADOPT

**2. OSCAL did not solve coverage. The assessment-results model has no unassessed state.**
CLAIM: `finding-target` status state allows only `satisfied` and `not-satisfied`. Observation `type` allows `ssp-statement-issue`, `control-objective`, `mitigation`, `discovery`, `historic` (`finding` deprecated). Risk `status` allows `open`, `investigating`, `remediating`, `deviation-requested`, `deviation-approved`, `closed`. No token means "not assessed", "not applicable", or "unable to assess". · SOURCE: `oscal_assessment-common_metaschema.xml` / NIST OSCAL, main branch / https://raw.githubusercontent.com/usnistgov/OSCAL/main/src/metaschema/oscal_assessment-common_metaschema.xml (fetched) · RELEVANCE: Load-bearing negative. Having adopted the OSCAL observation/finding split, the project cannot inherit an `incomplete` outcome from it. It must define one, and it should borrow from XCCDF (finding 1) rather than from OSCAL. · VERDICT: NEGATIVE

**3. OSCAL puts scope exclusion in the plan, not the results.**
CLAIM: The exclusion machinery is `include-all`, `exclude-control`, `exclude-objective`, `exclude-subject`, plus `assessment-subject-placeholder` ("used when the assessment subjects will be determined as part of one or more other assessment activities"). These are assessment-*plan* constructs, not results constructs. · SOURCE: same metaschema, above (fetched) · RELEVANCE: OSCAL's shape is *declare scope in advance, report verdicts after*. That matches "declared roots" precisely, and argues the coverage manifest is two artifacts, not one: declared scope (authored input) and realized scope (observed output). The delta between them is the finding. · VERDICT: ADAPT

**4. The real analogue for discovered coverage loss is the audit scope limitation, graded by pervasiveness.**
CLAIM: When the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence: material but not pervasive → qualified opinion, worded "except for the *possible* effects"; material and pervasive → disclaimer, "we do not express an opinion". · SOURCE: ISA 705 (Revised) / IAASB, via IRBA copy / https://www.irba.co.za/upload/ISA-705-Revised.pdf ; AU-C 705 is the AICPA equivalent · RELEVANCE: Supplies a two-level escalation the project lacks. Per-rule `incomplete` is the qualified case. A *report-level* disclaimer is needed when omissions are pervasive enough that no verdict is honest — the tool should be able to refuse to render a summary line. · VERDICT: ADOPT

**5. SOC 2 carve-out is a scoping decision, not a limitation record — but the disclosure obligation transfers well.**
CLAIM: Under the carve-out method the subservice organization's controls are excluded from both the system description and the examination scope, but the description must still state the services performed by that organization, the Complementary Subservice Organization Controls (CSOCs) expected to be in place there, and the service organization's own controls for monitoring it. CSOCs are a disclosure, not a tested assertion. CUECs are controls management assumed the *user entity* would implement, necessary in combination with the service organization's controls to meet its service commitments. · SOURCE: practitioner summaries of AT-C 320 / SSAE 18 — Linford & Co (https://linfordco.com/blog/subservice-carve-out-inclusive-audits/), Schneider Downs, MACPAS, CompassITC, 2023-2025. No AICPA primary text fetched. · RELEVANCE: Confirms your hypothesis on the *printed, not hidden* point, and gives the manifest entry shape: name the omission, state what would have to be true outside the scan for the verdict to hold, state what the tool does to detect the gap. · VERDICT: ADAPT

**6. No control framework requires proving inventory completeness, and CIS does not measure the unknown.**
CLAIM: The CIS Controls Assessment Specification scores Safeguard 1.1 with an Accuracy score (percentage of the aggregate endpoint inventory accounted for) and a Completeness score (percentage of inventory entries carrying the required detail fields). "Completeness" there means field-level detail, not population coverage. No measure of undiscovered assets exists; the approved inventory is treated as authoritative. · SOURCE: CIS Controls Assessment Specification v8.1, Control 1 / Center for Internet Security / https://cas.docs.cisecurity.org/en/latest/source/Controls1/ (fetched) · RELEVANCE: The most-cited asset framework quietly redefines "complete" as "well-described". Do not reuse the word. Name the metric for what it measures — declared-root coverage — or the project repeats the same equivocation it exists to expose. · VERDICT: NEGATIVE

**7. Access recertification non-response defaults to *retain*, not revoke.**
CLAIM: At campaign completion, undecided items are handled per campaign configuration: maintain access, or revoke. Vendor guidance is explicit: "SailPoint strongly recommends you choose to maintain access to undecided items due to the difficulty of reinstating access after it is revoked." No automatic disposition fires without an administrator completing the campaign. · SOURCE: "Completing a Certification Campaign" / SailPoint Identity Security Cloud documentation / undated / https://documentation.sailpoint.com/saas/help/certs/completing_campaigns.html (fetched) · RELEVANCE: Contradicts the dispatcher's revoke hypothesis. The transferable rule is narrower and better: a non-decision is recorded as a non-decision and is never silently converted into a verdict in either direction. · VERDICT: ADAPT

**8. Vulnerability scanning treats unscanned assets as a scope defect, not a first-class result type.**
CLAIM: FedRAMP requires that "the entire inventory (or approved sampling percentage) within the boundary must be scanned at the operating system (OS) level at least once a month", with an automated mechanism to catalog all in-boundary assets monthly "to ensure that everything is being scanned appropriately". The page states no requirement to report assets that could not be scanned. · SOURCE: Vulnerability Scanning / FedRAMP legacy playbook / https://www.fedramp.gov/legacy/playbook/csp/continuous-monitoring/vulnerability-scanning/ (fetched; the canonical v3.0 PDF at fedramp.gov/resources/documents/CSP_Vulnerability_Scanning_Requirements.pdf returned HTTP 404) · RELEVANCE: Coverage is enforced by reconciling scanner output against an independently maintained inventory. The scanner is not trusted to declare its own coverage. Same structure applies here: declared roots are the ledger, the scan reconciles against them, and the manifest is the reconciliation — not the scanner's self-report. · VERDICT: ADAPT

## What the dispatching session got wrong or missed

**The carve-out is the wrong analogue for the coverage manifest.** A carve-out is chosen before the examination starts — a boundary the auditor draws deliberately and prints. The coverage manifest records something different: limits discovered *during* the scan (rate limits, permission denials, truncated pagination). The standard for that is the scope limitation (ISA/AU-C 705), which the brief did not mention. Both are needed and they are different artifacts: declared roots are the carve-out, hit limits are the scope limitation. Conflating them produces one flat list that hides which omissions were intended.

**"Unprovable coverage" is not the exceptional case the brief treats it as.** No framework surveyed proves population completeness. CIS ships a metric named "Completeness" that measures field detail instead. FedRAMP asserts 100% coverage by reconciling against a ledger someone else maintains, which relocates the unprovability rather than removing it. The project's honesty here is normal engineering practice, not a novel burden — but it means "coverage" needs a printed definition on every report, because the field has already devalued the word.

**Four outcomes is probably too few.** XCCDF, in production since 2005, needed four distinct non-verdicts. This project's `inapplicable` conflates XCCDF `notapplicable` (rule does not apply to this target) with `notselected` (rule out of scope). Its `incomplete` conflates `notchecked` (never attempted) with `error`/`unknown` (attempted, failed). Recommend testing the merge against a real report before locking the enum.

**One thing the brief got right.** The instinct that non-verdicts must be printed rather than dropped is supported by every source surveyed. XCCDF goes one step further, and that step is the mechanism worth copying: a rule that could not be evaluated must not silently improve or worsen the pass rate.
